Executive Summary

Immigration law in Alberta is a shared provincial-federal operating function. Alberta may assess and nominate eligible candidates through the Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP), while the Government of Canada retains authority over immigration admission, temporary status, permanent residence, admissibility and citizenship. Outcomes depend on the pathway, Alberta labour-market and economic-development criteria, federal eligibility, timing, documentary sufficiency and the applicant's actual circumstances.

Operationally, Alberta immigration matters begin with route selection and jurisdictional allocation. An applicant or adviser must distinguish provincial nomination from federal immigration processing and determine whether the case concerns AAIP worker streams, Alberta Express Entry, Rural Renewal, a sector pathway, entrepreneur streams, study, family sponsorship, federal temporary status, permanent residence or another recognised route.

The Alberta immigration system is provincial-federal, document-driven and procedure-sensitive. AAIP assesses provincial nomination against Alberta criteria, but a provincial nomination does not itself grant Canadian permanent residence; the nominee must proceed through the relevant federal IRCC process and remain admissible under Canadian law.

Cross-border relevance is significant because Alberta immigration planning may interact with federal Canadian processing, international qualifications, employer group structures, provincial nomination criteria, Canadian tax residence, educational planning and future relocation options beyond Alberta itself.

Purpose

The purpose of immigration law structuring in Alberta is to align an applicant's provincial nomination, skills, employment, business, education or settlement profile with the correct federal Canadian immigration pathway. It exists to reduce the risk of provincial route mismatch, documentary failure, lost nomination opportunity, federal refusal or strategic mismatch between the applicant's objectives and the route selected.

A coherent Alberta immigration law position, including the correct AAIP or federal pathway, proper provincial and IRCC preparation, documentary support, procedural compliance and practical alignment with the person's intended work, business, study, settlement and long-term Canadian mobility objectives.

7. Nomination, Federal Decision and ContinuityImplement the nomination and federal decision, maintain temporary status where needed and prepare settlement, work, business, professional, family and long-term Canadian continuity.
Decision Tree

The decision tree distinguishes Alberta provincial nomination from federal Canadian immigration decisions. It is presented as a workflow so the reader does not mistake an AAIP nomination for a final grant of Canadian permanent residence.

  1. Identify the applicant's nationality, location, current federal Canadian status or application position, Alberta connection and intended activity.
  2. Confirm whether the intended pathway is an AAIP worker stream, Alberta Express Entry, Rural Renewal, an entrepreneur stream, employer-supported migration, study, family sponsorship, federal temporary status, permanent residence or another lawful route.
  3. Check whether the facts support Alberta provincial eligibility, intended settlement, sector or rural alignment and the required federal Canadian criteria.
  4. Determine whether an expression of interest, invitation, employer or community support, business proposal, federal work or study status, family sponsorship or admissibility issue creates a procedural constraint.
  5. Prepare a coherent provincial and federal file that matches the route selected.
  6. Maintain temporary status and plan for federal permanent residence, Alberta settlement, professional licensing or another lawful next step where needed.
