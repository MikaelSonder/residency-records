Executive Summary

Immigration law affecting Washington is principally federal: visas, admission, immigration status, employment authorisation, permanent residence and naturalisation are governed by United States federal law and federal authorities. The Washington-level function concerns how federal immigration status interfaces with state employment, professional licensing, Individual Taxpayer Identification Number use, refugee and immigrant assistance, education, health and local settlement realities.

Operationally, a Washington matter begins by separating the federal immigration route from the state-level implementation issue. An applicant or adviser must identify the relevant federal pathway—such as employment, family, study, humanitarian protection, permanent residence or naturalisation—and then assess Washington-specific consequences for professional licensing, employment, public assistance, ITIN use, identity documents, residence or settlement needs.

The Washington environment is document-driven and agency-specific. Outcomes can turn on the accuracy of federal immigration records, evidence of Washington residence, employment documentation, professional-licensing requirements, ITIN or Social Security number treatment and the practical coordination of federal and state processes.

Cross-border relevance is significant because Washington immigration planning may interact with federal visa processes abroad, multinational employer structures, international qualifications, cross-border family evidence, federal and Washington tax residence, educational planning and future relocation options beyond Washington itself.

Official English NameU.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS); Washington Office of Refugee and Immigrant Assistance (ORIA); Washington State licensing authorities
